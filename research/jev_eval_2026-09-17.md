# Jev 1.13 live evaluation — 2026-09-17

## Verdict

Jev is useful as a **shadow semantic prefilter** for opportunity routing. It is
fast, extremely cheap, accepts many typed questions against one state, and
correctly routed the six archived selectivity cases in this initial test. It
is **not validated as a standalone market-probability model**. Its five-market
forecast probabilities were far below the previously graded research-model
references, and small changes to the Apple state/question framing moved its
forecast materially. Keep probability formation, arithmetic, material-alert
suppression and asset actions outside Jev until a much larger prospective
calibration set says otherwise.

The benchmark begins **after** the caller has retrieved criteria, books, fees
and relevant facts and assembled them into a state. Jev has no browsing,
retrieval or tool execution, and cannot identify a decisive fact that the
caller omitted. The measured latency and spend are therefore marginal
inference figures, not end-to-end opportunity-analysis figures. Jev adds value
only where existing collectors already produce a sufficiently complete,
current state or where many atomic questions can amortize that preparation.
Retrieval, verification and state assembly may dominate the workflow and must
be included in any later speed, cost or ROI comparison with frontier research.

The operator-provided credential is stored only in
`~/.polyclaude/env` as `POLYCLAUDE_JEV_API_KEY`; the file remains mode 0600.
No key or request state is stored in the repository.

## Service and documented constraints

- Endpoint: `POST https://api.typesafe.ai/v1/systemone`; account access was
  confirmed with `GET /v1/models`.
- Pinned test model: `jev-1.13.0`. The moving aliases `jev-latest` and
  `jev-preview` were also enabled on the account.
- Question types: yes/no probability (`noul`), categorical choice, and ordered
  rubric score. Questions sharing one state execute in parallel.
- Price: **$0.042 per million input tokens; output is free**.
- Public limits: 250,000 input tokens/second and 1,200 requests/minute, subject
  to change.
- The vendor describes Jev as a System One model. Its own jaggedness guide says
  it is literal and weak on numeric precision, date comparison, indirection,
  irrelevant context and generation. Arithmetic and dates belong in code.
- Inputs are collected by the service. Its privacy policy says inputs are not
  used to train or fine-tune models, but public market information should still
  be the only state sent from this project.

Sources: [API reference](https://docs.typesafe.ai/api),
[models and pricing](https://docs.typesafe.ai/models),
[primitives](https://docs.typesafe.ai/primitives),
[model jaggedness](https://docs.typesafe.ai/model-jaggedness/jev-1.13), and

## Live measurements

Across **76 authenticated inference calls**, every request succeeded. Total
input was **47,982 tokens**, costing approximately **$0.002015** at the posted
rate.

| Test | Calls | Wall time | Per-call latency | Cost |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Detailed Apple state, five questions | 1 | 0.76 s | 0.76 s | $0.000040 |
| Apple stability, ten-way concurrency | 20 | 1.73 s | p50 0.79 s; p95 0.94 s | $0.000575 |
| Five archived forecasts × five samples | 25 | 2.27 s | p50 0.76 s; p95 0.89 s | $0.000704 |
| Six selectivity routes × five samples | 30 | 2.39 s | p50 0.85 s; p95 0.99 s | $0.000695 |

The VM did not reproduce the vendor's advertised 70–500 ms range: inference
was normally 0.65–1.0 seconds and one call reached 1.16 seconds. It is still
much faster at scoring an already-prepared state than a browsing/reasoning
agent. This is not an end-to-end comparison: the benchmark excluded retrieval,
state assembly and verification. Twenty-five independent forecast calls
completed in 2.27 seconds with concurrency.

At roughly 1,000 input tokens per candidate, 1,000 shadow scores would cost
about **$0.042**. One request can ask several atomic questions without paying
for the shared state again.

## Probability test

Five archived states from the July Kimi evaluation were reduced to dated
criteria plus already-verified facts. Each was sampled five times. The
reference column is the previously graded Kimi fair probability, not ground
truth; the comparison measures whether Jev can substitute for that research
step.

| Case | Research reference | Jev mean | Jev SD | Range |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bears permanent move | .55 | .084 | .005 | .08–.09 |
| Iran/Hormuz fees | .52 | .264 | .015 | .25–.29 |
| Cyclosporiasis ≥5,000 | .85 | .294 | .014 | .27–.31 |
| Touchscreen MacBook | .40 | .226 | .005 | .22–.23 |
| SpaceX highest IPO cap | .96 | .670 | .011 | .65–.68 |

Mean absolute disagreement was **.348**. Jev was internally stable but
systematically conservative about prospective events. That stable bias is not
useful until calibrated on a larger, prospectively frozen, resolved sample.

Prompt/state sensitivity also matters. A more detailed Apple state produced
P(YES)=.39, while a compact state with the same core evidence averaged .224.
On 20 compact repeats, the action choice was HOLD 16 times and SELL four times;
choice confidence was low. This rules out using one raw Jev probability or
action label in portfolio logic.

## Opportunity-routing test

The archived Part-B selectivity set supplied criteria, price context and one
verified fact per market. Ground truth had one fact-linked candidate (Sulyok)
and five rejects (WTI carry, Hormuz traffic carry, Fed consensus, prospective
French transit, and speculative Prime/SDCC). Five samples per case produced:

- **30/30 stable route labels** and **6/6 correct majority routes**.
- Sulyok routed to `candidate` in all five samples.
- Every other case routed to `reject` in all five samples.
- Some raw `instance_edge` probabilities were misleadingly high for WTI and
  Hormuz traffic even though the richer categorical route was correct. Consume
  the defined routing label and its confidence; do not infer trade EV from a
  generic noul.

This is the promising use: Jev can cheaply decide which fully specified public
states deserve expensive research. It did not discover or verify the facts;
those were supplied to it.

## Safe architecture

1. Deterministic code fetches identity, criteria, books, fees and dates and
   performs all arithmetic. Jev runs only after this retrieval step.
2. Existing source collectors attach dated public snippets or structured
   facts and a completeness contract identifies required fields and freshness.
   Missing or stale inputs fail open to frontier review; a Jev output cannot
   turn an incomplete state into a rejection. No private wallet, operator or
   credential material is sent.
3. Jev answers atomic semantic questions in one batch: causal relevance,
   literal criteria match, evidence class, ambiguity and review route.
4. During shadowing, `candidate` and `research` may promote an item to the
   frontier pipeline. `reject` cannot suppress a material alert or candidate;
   it is measured only.
5. Frontier research sets probabilities and makes every portfolio decision.
   Existing entry, fee, robust-EV, cap and transaction gates remain unchanged.

Collect at least 200–500 prospectively frozen shadow rows with provider/model,
prompt version, exact input hash, retrieval and assembly latency/cost, state
completeness, Jev latency/token cost, Jev outputs, frontier route and eventual
outcome. Measure end-to-end candidate recall first, then precision and
probability calibration. Promotion should require high recall on the costly
miss class and a clean sample of strict-criteria traps. Pin the model version;
retest before changing it.
