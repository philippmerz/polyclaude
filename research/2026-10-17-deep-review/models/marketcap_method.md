# Primary model for the four company-rank conditions

Dated October17,2026. This is a shared model, not four independent signals. Each exact condition has its own report, probability and economic decision. The source packet is `data/deep_review_20261017/market_cap_history_sources.json`; tracked inputs, computation and results are alongside this file. Nothing here is an order or a validated tail bound.

The full rules specify a company rank at market close and consensus credible reporting. October31 is a Saturday. The main model uses the final regular October30 close,10 trading sessions after October16; the literal weekend/alternate-market interpretation is an unresolved venue fork. December31 is a regular US trading date;52 remaining sessions exclude Thanksgiving and Christmas. An extraordinary exchange closure, reporting lag or dispute can change timing.

The [Nasdaq historical endpoint](https://api.nasdaq.com/api/quote/MSFT/historical?assetclass=stocks&fromdate=2026-06-01&todate=2026-10-16&limit=120) supplied90 common closing sessions June10–October16 for NVDA,AAPL,MSFT,GOOGL,GOOG. Source rows are retained in the tracked input. Close/Last is price, not total return. Issuer split histories have no split in this interval; dividends are not added to company capitalization. Do not confuse a quote endpoint's PreviousClose field with the latest closing price. October16 closes used here are214.13,343.46,529.05,340.59 and336.91 respectively.

Computation uses89 daily joint log-return vectors and their empirical covariance. Daily standard deviations are NVDA2.302%,Apple1.776%,Microsoft2.327%,Alphabet2.052%; correlations are in the input file and are not assumed equal or independent. We center returns instead of extrapolating a90-day rally. Five methods use200,000 joint terminal draws each, seed20261017:

- Gaussian zero-log-drift covariance scaling.
- Centered whole-vector daily bootstrap, preserving observed cross-company day shocks but assuming independent future days.
- Gaussian plus independent company earnings/log shocks: short horizon SD3%,0%,8%,8% for NVDA,Apple,Microsoft,Alphabet; December10%,6%,8%,8%. These are primary sensitivity assumptions, not option-implied event moves.
- Wider jumps, multiplying those event SDs by1.875.
- Terminal Student-t5 scale mixture normalized to the same variance. This is a sensitivity distribution, not a calibrated dynamical process.

Only four companies are simulated; a large outsider rise or top-company fall is covered qualitatively and in the case ranges. Share-count, legal/company aggregation, earnings dependence, asymmetric jump risk, empirical-window instability, future drift and option-implied information are not identified by this small sample. Earnings shocks can double count part of historical event volatility; their point is to expose tail sensitivity rather than claim extra precision. Neither simulation sample count nor a tight Monte Carlo standard error makes the model's tail probabilities reliable. No unconditional lower bound or measured-tail execution exception follows from it.

The short Microsoft-first probability varies0.0215–3.894% across methods; December Microsoft-first2.568–6.91%; short Alphabet-first0.085–7.123%; Apple-exact-third1.481–13.582%. Central case judgments emphasize the earnings-jump model, rounded broadly and accompanied by wider ranges. These numerical ranges are **model outputs**, not real-world calibrated confidence intervals. Each report explains its own entry threshold, missing information and both-sided price economics.
