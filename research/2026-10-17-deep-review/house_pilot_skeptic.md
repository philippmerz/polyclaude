# Skeptic review: ME02 YES and CA06 NO

Read-only review of primary evaluations for frozen indices 123 and 32, their settlement clauses, source packets and the transparent-but-uncalibrated house model. This challenges edge estimates; it is not a veto or alternate forecast.

**ME02 YES, 10 shares at no more than $0.39.** The official ballot confirms Dunlap (D) versus LePage (R). The structural prior is a D margin of −0.4 points: national D+8, translated R+4 PVI (−8 margin) at β=.8, then a −2 candidate adjustment. Tulchin's Oct 12–14 likely-voter poll (Dunlap 50, LePage 46, undecided 4; n=500; ±4.38pp) plus a 1.5-point anti-D sensitivity produces +1.37 margin, SD 4.75 and 61% D. Its normal precision update combines prior SD 7.62 and poll SD 6.08; these uncertainty choices are not calibrated. The prior, β, adjustment, common-error and poll-bias terms are judgments, not fitted effects. The poll memo names no client and omits mode, wording/order, weighting and likely-voter screen; its four-point lead is within the reported candidate-share MOE. UNH's primary abstract says “statistically tied,” but its numeric attachment was inaccessible; do not turn the secondary 51–45 into independent precision.

History is mixed: same-map 2024 president was Trump 53.50%–Harris 44.46%, while Golden narrowly beat Theriault in final RCV (50.35%–49.65%). Golden's crossover is not Dunlap's, and LePage's often-repeated 2022 CD2 governor win lacks an official district tabulation. Q3 FEC filings show Dunlap raised more ($1.466m vs $0.669m) but had less cash ($738k vs $1.195m); neither is a vote model. At $0.39, the 10-point haircut leaves $0.12/share gross ($1.20 total) before costs. The primary's own range starts at 15% D. A maker may fill when new information has weakened the thesis.

**CA06 NO, about 50 shares at no more than $0.033.** NO wins if the seat winner is not classified Democratic. Pan is D; Kiley is NPP under the new Prop 50 map. The frozen rule assigns an unaffiliated winner by most recent caucus intent when all House races are called. Kiley's reported intent not to caucus R is not a promise to caucus D; no future-D intent was found. The 85% D estimate translates D+5 PVI and national D+8, applies −2 incumbency and −4 candidate adjustments, then updates on a Sep 1–6 Bullfinch poll (Pan 36.7, Kiley 36.5, DK 26.7; n=927; reported ±3.2pp) with +1.5 Pan bias and 4pp age/common-error terms. This normal update has poll SD 6.53 and posterior SD 4.96; it consumes the candidate margin, not the 26.7% undecided allocation. The poll was already 41 days old on Oct17; adding age variance without mean decay is a judgment. Coefficients and the model's 2% Kiley-to-D affiliation branch are uncalibrated. Poll mode/questionnaire are unavailable and the sponsor is Kiley-aligned. Its related July poll had Pan +4 and 26% undecided; September was nearly tied. That is not independent evidence, but argues against assuming a stable Pan lead. The new map has no transferable prior House result; current Q3 campaign-finance totals were not captured.

At 3.3¢, break-even is 3.3% before costs; the 10-point haircut leaves 5%, or 1.7¢/share gross ($0.85 total) before carry/operations. At the unhaircut 15%, central gross EV is $5.85 on 50 shares. A taker-only schedule means no maker fee; rounding to 3¢ changes fill odds. A modest miss erases the thin stressed edge. Resting fills are selected, so unconditional 15% may not hold conditional on a fill. Both contracts share national-election error and the all-House-call resolution trigger, while their local risks differ; use joint cluster exposure rather than treating these edges as independent.
