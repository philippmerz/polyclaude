# Case 0112 — independent champion and counter-thesis advisory

Advisory only; the primary operator retains the valuation and decision. This review uses the canonical evaluation and its dated source packet, not an additional market or account read.

## Strongest case for NO

The official OpenRouter weekly view observed at 10:30 UTC on October 18 showed a rolling-period API sum of 168.339T tokens across 649 model-variant rows. Four unchanged weekly equivalents are 673.357T, below the market’s inclusive 725T threshold. Reaching the threshold requires roughly 7.7% growth in the four-week sum from that reference level. If aggregate use stays near the observed run rate, or contracts, the fixed-window total lands below the bracket and NO wins. The primary’s steady/disruption components and 9% central YES express this thesis.

The case for NO is therefore coherent: a high bar relative to a recent rolling-week snapshot, with only a short gap before the fixed settlement window begins. It is not independently calibrated. The rolling chart is not the settlement series, and the available public API does not provide the required historical Monday–Sunday values or the chart’s later revision path.

## Strongest counter-thesis: YES or cash

The contract needs only modest aggregate growth to enter the band, and the top-model table includes rapidly changing model releases and usage shares. A common regime can move all four weeks together; four weeks should not be treated as independent observations. The primary’s own post-quote sensitivity demonstrates the central risk: a distribution concentrated at 750T with log-sigma 0.03 places about 73.36% in the 725–775T bracket; at sigma 0.05 it is about 49.51%, and at sigma 0.10 about 26.12%. Thus the price does not itself establish a NO edge. With historical fixed-week outcomes unavailable, the original sigmas and scenario weights (including 40% steady, 40% adoption, 15% disruption, 5% source/chart uncertainty) remain judgmental rather than fitted. A YES thesis can be built from continued adoption plus relatively low aggregate volatility, but the public source packet does not establish that volatility assumption either. Cash is a defensible alternative until the central regime and dispersion are independently supported.

## What is known, and what remains an assumption

- The frozen rule sums displayed Top Models weekly totals for October 26, November 2, November 9, and November 16; weeks run Monday 00:00 UTC through Sunday 23:59 UTC. The values are taken when the November 23 week first appears, or at 23:59 ET November 27 if it has not appeared. Display rounding counts; an exact bracket boundary resolves to the higher bracket. The contract window had not started at the October 18 source capture.
- OpenRouter’s captured page describes “This Week” as a trailing seven-day window through the latest complete UTC bucket. Its API response contains one row per model variant with a date label, not a daily panel. The verified 168.339T is a computed sum of ranking fields, not a directly displayed total and not a settlement figure. The displayed DeepSeek row changed from 36.8T in the prior snapshot to 35.9T in the October 18 snapshot; that is model-specific and does not establish aggregate decline.
- The source packet’s 168.339T baseline and 673.357T four-week equivalent are usable reference arithmetic, not a forecasted settlement. The API’s date labels cannot be divided into daily rates. Documented historical daily data required authentication (401); no authentication or bypass was attempted. Prior public date-parameter probes did not recover fixed-week history.
- A primary page later in the period could revise prior weekly figures. No observed revision distribution or exact rounding algorithm was available. The rule’s first-display/fallback timing is explicit, but the accessible evidence does not establish precisely how the chart will expose these fixed calendar weeks or whether the public weekly API is exactly binding at that future display. That uncertainty could be material near 725T or 775T.

## Economics and decision sensitivity

The canonical report’s October 17 quote is stale for execution: NO was bid 0.64 / offered 0.67, with a 10-share offer cost of $6.78844 including recorded fees. At its 91% central NO probability, the report calculated $2.31156 expected profit before carry/operations; under its selected 76% NO stress, $0.81156; under a 20% NO sensitivity, a $4.78844 loss. Five NO shares at a 0.64 maker price were separately described as $3.20 risk, $1.35 central EV, and $0.60 selected-stress EV before carry/operations. These are dated scenarios, not current market terms or an execution certificate.

The 15-percentage-point reserve is not a substitute for the broad 0.5–80% sensitivity range or source calibration. A small entry is only defensible if an independent reviewer accepts the NO-side central regime despite the concentrated-750T countermodel, and current rule identity, UMA status, fees, books, account, minimum, liquidity, carry, and correlated OpenRouter/HLE exposure all pass at decision time. Otherwise cash preserves option value while the fixed window and initial weekly observations approach. Any live proposal should use fresh observations and monitor adoption, aggregate displayed totals, model changes, and chart methodology; it should not use this advisory as approval.
