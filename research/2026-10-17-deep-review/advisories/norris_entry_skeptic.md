This is an advisory, not a replacement probability, execution approval, or order.

## Strongest case for a tiny YES

Norris died March 19, was internationally recognized through film and martial arts, and has a persistent meme identity. A death may create a large new search shock against the prior-year comparison, so the model is right to consider him seriously while rejecting “most famous person wins” as its rule. The last five Global Passings winners were DMX, Queen Elizabeth, Matthew Perry, Liam Payne, and Charlie Kirk; they show winners can be entertainment figures, a monarch, or a political-news shock. They do **not** estimate Norris’s win rate.

At the stated 3¢ cap, a 34-share passive fill costs $1.02 before operational/carry costs. If the model’s selected 5% stress probability were correct, gross expected profit is $0.68; at its 25% center it is $7.48. A single tiny ticket is the most defensible positive case because downside is capped and the thesis is at least event-grounded. But 5% is also the bottom of the 5–65% range and leaves little room for fees, gas, or estimation error. At 1¢, 102 shares costs the same $1.02 but a fill is less likely and more exposed to adverse selection. These are conditional calculations, not evidence that a fill or edge will occur.

## Reasons the current case is fragile

- Google’s captured method is for 2025 only: Jan. 1–Nov. 25, compared with the same prior-year window, using internal tools to find fastest-rising search traffic. It gives no reproducible growth formula, no 2026 cutoff, and no absolute-volume/rank series. The March 19 death could create a strong rise from a low comparison baseline, but no observed 2026 global series tests that mechanism. Historical category winners are only five observations, and past methodology/cutoffs are not verified identical for all five years.
- The named contender ledger is incomplete. It allocates 10% to “other current” and 18% to later/unobserved winners, but these are judgmental buckets, not an inventory or estimated arrival process. Known cases already include globally recognized Dolly Parton, Ali Khamenei, and Alex Pretti; the source index also includes other 2026 deaths such as Terry Yorath and Franco Baresi. The categories and countries are heterogeneous. A missing death, transliteration, alias, or search-term/entity mapping can materially change which person tops a growth list.
- The rule requires rank #1, not Top 5. A strong global spike can still lose to one competing eligible death. Conversely, U.S.-specific salience for Pretti is not proof of global rank. It is sensible to retain uncertainty, but the 25% center and the exact 25/15/20/10/10/18/2 allocation are unsupported by measured relative search growth.
- The missing-list branch explicitly resolves to “Other” after March 31, 2027, while ordinary publication may be in December. The market end-date of Dec. 31 is not the rule cutoff. Before any order, verify the exact child/parent and UMA payout mapping for “Other” and any absent/late-list branch; do not treat release delay as either an automatic Norris NO or a long-duration free option without confirming settlement mechanics. Publication could precede the fallback by months, so scheduled monitoring must be ready to cancel or reassess before the official list is known, not after.
- The quoted 0.9¢ bid / 11¢ ask was captured Oct. 17, with books 118 seconds old at receipt. The compact fee descriptor was missing, though the captured structured Gamma schedule said 5% linear and taker-only. A passive-maker candidate avoids that taker curve only if the live descriptor, fee treatment, share minimum/tick, strict asset mapping, and current books are revalidated. Small size limits dollars at risk but does not repair the information gap.

## Crux and falsifiers

The crux is whether Norris’s death generated enough **global year-over-year search growth** to beat every other person who died in the qualifying 2026 window—not whether he is famous or whether the event went viral in the United States. The central estimate would be falsified downward by a verified Google methodology/window that excludes or materially limits the March event; comparable global data showing a weaker Norris rise than another eligible person; a more dominant international competitor; or an official list placing anyone else first. A verified Google query/entity definition or relative-growth series favoring Norris, plus an official cutoff/list process and exact Other settlement path, would materially strengthen the tiny YES case. None is in the reviewed source set.

**Conclusion:** the upside arithmetic can justify investigating a very small passive entry, but source uncertainty—not ordinary sampling noise—dominates. I would not treat the existing 25% as independently validated; require exact settlement mapping and fresh execution inputs, and preserve the full $1.02 loss case. No new probability or action is proposed here.
