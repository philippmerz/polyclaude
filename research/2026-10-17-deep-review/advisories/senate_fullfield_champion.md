# Independent champion audit — full-field Senate model

The updated model is decision-useful as a transparent scenario map of all 35 seats, and it contains a credible pro-D control path. The strongest case for using it is that the source coverage now reaches every contest and brings recent, candidate-specific polling into most close states rather than relying only on presidential baselines. The model also keeps special elections in the 35-seat count, corrects the D-aligned/R non-ballot baseline to 34/31, treats independent winners separately, and makes the Senate-control and first-Pro-Tem branches explicit. Its central run is 60,000 draws; Monte Carlo noise is small beside the stated parameter uncertainty.

Several fresh sources matter to the case for a competitive Senate: Florida now uses Angie Nixon rather than the stale Vindman matchup; Kansas has two current original polls; South Carolina has current original polls; Arkansas has an Oct. 15 full-field TBP–Hendrix release; and Louisiana’s latest Hart row and prior PPP report are carried with their weaker provenance identified. Additional current evidence covers Georgia, Minnesota, Alaska, and Nebraska, with source limits preserved (including the inaccessible original Alaska poll and unresolved Minnesota chart method). Where a source is only a directory attribution or a secondary account, the input marks it and limits its poll weight or adds source uncertainty. The six states with no matched poll in the reviewed directory remain in the model rather than being dropped.

The pro-D-control thesis is not driven by one deterministic toss-up: the model combines a national environment prior, documented current polling, and candidate-specific evidence in Ohio and Alaska. Starting from the corrected 34 D-aligned / 31 R nonballot seats, D needs 17 of the 35 contests to reach 51 aligned seats. The model produces 56.8% D control centrally and 52.3% for D Senate plus D House, while retaining 1.1% “other Senate control.” A very small maker order could be rational if the live contract price leaves adequate expected value after fees and execution risk, because the model exposes a broad evidence base and can be stress-tested before committing capital. This audit did not inspect venue prices and does not recommend an order or assign an alternative fair value.

The main limit is calibration, not simulation precision. The results are explicitly not fitted, backtested, or calibrated. The central D-control result moves to 67.0% if mean poll bias is zero and to 46.1% at −3 points; generic national margin D6/D10 moves it to 50.5%/63.1%. Those are one-at-a-time sensitivities, not a joint credible interval. The assumed 75% retention of OH/AK personal overperformance moves control from 49.3% with none to 59.2% with full retention. These are material modeling judgments, so a small entry needs a meaningful price cushion rather than an edge inferred from the central point alone.

Coalition uncertainty remains an independent decision risk. NE is modeled as an Independent winner with only 35% central D caucus probability, 10% R, and 55% neither; the model does not claim a current D pledge. ID and SD also have no-caucus intent evidence plus judgmental future-reversal allowances; Idaho’s competing poll regimes are assigned equal weight despite one being only a directory attribution. Montana explicitly allows R/I/D/L winners, but its 10% consolidation regime is not empirical. Moving all new independents to D raises D control to 63.6%, while assigning none to D gives 56.3%; routing ambiguous 50–50 outcomes to R versus D changes the result from 51.6% to 63.0%. The ordinary 50–50 tie favors R via the VP, but a no-majority Pro Tem may be neither party. These branches are decision-relevant, not cosmetic.

The joint House/Senate figures inherit an assumed 80% House marginal and a tunable shared cycle loading: D Senate/D House ranges from 48.8% to 53.9% across the displayed loading sensitivity, even though the Senate marginal stays fixed. That is a useful correlation disclosure, not empirical validation. Before relying on any close-price edge, the primary should check the exact contract settlement wording, quote quality, and whether its selected control/caucus branch matches that wording; it should also show joint adverse poll-bias, generic-environment, candidate-retention, and independent-caucus cases. The present sensitivity grid varies these mostly one at a time, so it may understate stacked uncertainty.

Source gaps do not erase the value of the exercise, but they constrain its resolution: the latest LA Hart instrument was not recovered; AK’s latest source is an attributed transcript and the original returned 403; MN’s chart remainder/method is unresolved; NE’s poll is an NPA memo and there is no D caucus pledge; and official candidate certification was not independently obtained for every low-salience seat. For those reasons the model is strongest as a disciplined map for a tiny, price-sensitive entry with a substantial margin of safety, not as a precise 56.8-cent quote.
