# ECB October decision — constructive-method audit

**Scope:** advisory on the pre-quote model in `models/ecb272_primary_2105.json`; not a replacement probability or trade judgment. Contract identity is market 3081965, condition `0xf97e…075ac`, criteria SHA `68ca4cf2…390a`. The frozen YES is *no change in the deposit facility rate resulting from the Oct 28–29 meeting*, versus its pre-meeting level. It excludes unrelated emergency changes; if rates move before the meeting, the reference level moves too.

There is a defensible causal route to materially more than the dated survey’s 18.7% average probability of any October-meeting change (81.3% hold): September HICP accelerated to 3.8% year over year and 0.6% month over month, while Lagarde’s Oct 17 statement said higher energy prices would keep headline inflation well above target into H1 2027 and risks were tilted upward. The September decision also refused to pre-commit to a path. A second hike is therefore a live reaction function, not an outcome ruled out by the September +25bp move. The model’s displayed branches imply roughly 30.5% for any change, mostly a hike; that is a coherent scenario argument, not a frequency estimate.

The bridge remains mostly judgment. The 65/25/10 regime weights, each regime’s hold/hike/cut response, and the 0.5% residual bins have no fitted sample or calibration evidence in the packet. The energy-broadening branch contributes most of the hike mass, but the evidence establishes an energy-driven headline shock and upside risk, not that the shock had broadened into domestic wages/core prices or that the Council would hike again within six weeks. September’s realized hike is not independent evidence of another hike: the SMA’s October row was explicitly a separate October-meeting question, not “second nonzero move.”

The reported mixture does reconcile once the model’s explicit 0.5% meeting-completion fallback to YES (no change) is applied: the regime mixture 69.50% hold, 26.05% hike, and 4.45% cut is multiplied by 99.5%, then the fallback is added to hold, yielding 69.6525%, 25.91975%, and 4.42775%. The arithmetic is sound; the 0.5% fallback is still a judgment rather than an estimated frequency, alongside the regime weights and conditional responses.

The strongest pause case is substantial: the September hike is already in force; Lagarde described broad underlying inflation as stable, core inflation at 2.5%, and no material wage response, while saying a measured response remains appropriate. The 81.3% hold estimate is dated (Aug 24–26) and not truth, but is a directly meeting-specific prior anchor. The October SMA questionnaire was blank and its aggregate is scheduled for Nov 2, after the decision, so there is no newer public survey update in these sources. A 90–97% hold view can follow if the Council treats the energy shock as temporary/pass-through, waits for transmission from September, and sees no near-term second-round effects. The present sources do not resolve that choice.

**Useful falsifiers before the meeting:** an ECB communication explicitly leaning toward a near-term further increase; domestic wage, services, or core-inflation evidence showing rapid broadening; or, in the other direction, evidence that the energy shock is fading or policy transmission is materially weakening. The October headline HICP flash is scheduled after the meeting, limiting one obvious update. The 15-point reserve acknowledges stale survey evidence and uncalibrated reaction assumptions, but it does not calibrate them or guarantee safety at every range endpoint.
