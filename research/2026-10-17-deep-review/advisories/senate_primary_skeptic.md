# Senate model skeptic review

**Scope:** Advisory audit of the primary-authored Senate/joint-control model, frozen rules for cases 10, 52 and 73, and the official FEC/Clerk baseline packets. This is not an alternate forecast or trade instruction.

## What checks out

- Seat arithmetic is internally consistent: 33 regular Class II seats (13 D-held, 20 R-held) plus the 2026 OH and FL special elections (both R-held) gives 35 contests (13 D/22 R). The remaining 65 seats are 34 D-aligned and 31 R, matching the current 47–53 Senate caucus after subtracting 2026 contests. The model’s 34+31 fixed seats and 35 simulated races sum to 100. It now asserts the 13-D/33-regular/OH+FL decomposition.
- The control code is consistent with case 52’s literal tie branches, assuming the Republican Vice President remains in office: D needs 51; R controls at 51, or at 50 when D has no majority. If neither branch resolves control, the rule names the first selected Pro Tempore. Case 73 uses the same Senate clause. “Caucus with everybody” is not reported as an announced D pledge in the correction packet; the model appropriately does not automatically count Osborn for D.
- The MOE conversion is defensible as a conservative approximation for close two-candidate polls: it uses twice the candidate-share MOE divided by 1.96 as a D–R standard error, then adds source and age uncertainty. It should remain labeled as an assumption for multi-candidate and nonprobability polls, not as exact poll variance.
- Personal-overperformance arithmetic in the inputs matches the same-source Clerk all-vote comparisons: OH Brown/Moreno vs. Ohio president, NE Osborn/Fischer vs. Nebraska president, and AK Peltola/Begich vs. Alaska president. The input explicitly flags AK as first-choice House support, not certified final-round RCV. These are valid descriptive differences, not portable candidate effects.

## Material weaknesses / update leads

- **Kansas is missing current evidence.** The model still has KS as a poll-free historical prior. Two original pollster reports identify the current survey choices as Adam Hamilton (D), Roger Marshall (R), and Libertarian David C. Graham. Trafalgar fielded Sept. 29–Oct. 1 (n=1,095 likely general-election voters): 44.4/43.5/2.0/10.1 undecided, ±2.9, 95%; Emerson/Nexstar fielded Sept. 15–16 (n=750 likely voters): 45/43/4/8, ±3.6 credibility interval. Trafalgar’s retrieved PDF has the topline chart and crosstabs; its detailed method page was not fetched. Emerson reports MMS voter-file text-to-web plus PureSpectrum panel interviews and weighting. Source bodies and request receipts are in `data/deep_review_20261017/senate_secondary_battlegrounds_ks.json` and `logs/deep_review_20261017/senate_secondary_battlegrounds_ks/`. These are not pooled here. This is a real live-state input gap; the primary should decide how to weight it before relying on chamber-level value.
- Model outputs are highly assumption-sensitive around control: primary sensitivity runs move D Senate control from 42.7% at a −3pp common poll bias to 60.5% at zero bias; generic D6/D10 runs are 43.6%/59.7%. Removing versus fully retaining the three personal-history adjustments moves it 43.3%/54.3%. These are the primary’s scenarios, not independent evidence. The evidence does not select the central −1.5pp poll bias, D8 generic ballot, 0.8 swing transfer, or 75% personal-retention factor. The current 2024 OH/NE/AK descriptive deltas involve different candidate types (incumbent Senator, independent challenger, incumbent House member) and are weak grounds for one common retention coefficient.
- Source denominators are carefully disclosed but not fully harmonized: the model’s national prior uses the FEC workbook, while OH/NE/AK personal comparisons use the Clerk’s same-source counts. FEC and Clerk national presidential totals differ by about 44,381 D−R votes (roughly 0.03pp); state totals also differ. The packet flags this. A consistent one-source baseline is preferable; this discrepancy is unlikely to dominate the model but should not be hidden.
- The 80% House-D marginal is imposed by a normal threshold, not derived from 435 district outcomes or the Speaker fallback in case 10. Case 73’s joint D-Senate/D-House output therefore depends on an assumed House marginal and chosen loading. In the primary’s own runs, changing the loading from .35 to .85 changes the joint D/D mass from 44.5% to 49.4% while leaving Senate marginal unchanged. That is a model-input decision, not an observed correlation.
- The no-pledge Osborn scenario follows the contract’s Jan. 4 fallback window, but `ne_d=.35`, `ne_r=.10`, and `protem_d=.50` are subjective. The 50% Pro Tem branch is the contract’s stated resolution mechanism, not an observable legal coin flip; selection may be endogenous to organization/control. Its simulated unresolved mass is small (~1.5%), so it is a secondary sensitivity rather than the leading flaw.

## Bottom line for the primary

No seat-count arithmetic or obvious tie-code bug found. The strongest decision-relevant correction is to incorporate or explicitly reject the two Kansas primary poll releases, then show the chamber/joint sensitivity to the selected poll-bias, generic-ballot and House-correlation assumptions. The model itself labels these as uncalibrated; the close central chamber result should be treated as conditional on those choices.
