# Case 260 skeptical review — 2026-10-18

The proposed 20¢ NO maker has little room after the 20-point reserve: the model’s 41.4659% central NO probability becomes 21.4659%, only 1.4659 points above a 20¢ price. At five shares, that is about 7.3¢ before carry and operating costs (and assuming the passive maker receives no fee). A modest probability shift, worse fill, or hidden execution cost erases it. This is a useful conditional arithmetic result, not a robust edge floor.

**No binding source clarification was found in the saved material** for what “October 31, 2026, as of market close” means on a Saturday, which market-cap vendor or currency conversion governs, whether Alphabet’s listed and unlisted classes are aggregated, or which outlets count as “a consensus of credible reporting.” The recorded assumption of the prior U.S. regular close on Friday Oct. 30 is sensible but remains an assumption. The saved CompaniesMarketCap snapshot is explicitly secondary; it is not evidence of a future Oct. 31 rank or of a reporting consensus. The old Oct. 16-to-Oct. 14 close error is corrected: case 260 uses verified Oct. 16 Nasdaq closes GOOGL $340.59 and GOOG $336.91.

**Semantic and universe risks can move an exact-third result sharply.** Alphabet’s Jun. 30 share counts are dated, not Oct. 31 counts; its Class B is unlisted, and the model values B like A. The captured vendor’s Alphabet cap is $4.120T versus $4.1451T in the issuer-share arithmetic. That difference does not by itself reverse today’s order, but it shows the market’s company-level convention is material if the ranking gap narrows. Current observations place NVDA and Apple above Alphabet and Microsoft below it, but an exact-third condition loses on either side: Alphabet can rise to first/second or fall below the two leaders. Amazon, TSMC, SpaceX, and other global issuers add outsider risk; the model assigns only a small subjective outsider branch. SpaceX is already treated as public in the source record.

**The event model is assumption-sensitive.** It uses 10 future sessions ending at assumed Oct. 30 close, fixed dated shares, zero log drift, equal weights on overlapping 20- and 89-session covariance windows, and subjective earnings shocks (8% log-SD for MSFT/Alphabet; 3% NVDA; Apple zero before its Nov. 2 call). Microsoft and Alphabet both report Oct. 28, so the conditional relative move is concentrated in one event window. The diagnostic’s central Alphabet exact-third result is 61.1215%; alternative saved setups span roughly 43.9–91.0%, before the separate joint fork. These are model scenarios, not independent empirical probabilities. The 5% consensus/calendar branch and 2% outsider branch are judgments, not fitted frequencies. A different shared shock, earnings covariance, October share count, or resolution vendor can consume the 1.47-point reserved margin.
