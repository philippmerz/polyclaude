# Case 262: constructive evidence review

This is an advisory on the existing model, not an alternate probability or market recommendation. I did not inspect quotes or economics.

The frozen rule resolves YES only if Chalamet ranks first in Google’s official 2026 **Global Actors** Year in Search list; if that list has not appeared by March 31, 2027, it resolves Other. The specified list controls, so the target is a global relative-growth rank, not absolute search volume.

The strongest case against abandoning YES is that Year in Search selects growth, not fame. The 2025 methodology describes lists of queries with the fastest increase in interest versus the same prior-year period. A publicity shock can therefore outrank a more famous actor with steady interest. Chalamet’s absence from the displayed 2023–25 Global Actors top tens is censored evidence: it gives no exact rank and does not establish zero growth. First-place names varied—Jeremy Renner, Katt Williams, Mikey Madison—consistent with event-driven attention rather than a stable “biggest star” list.

There are real 2026 catalysts: Chalamet’s Best Actor Oscar nomination and *Dune: Part Three*, which Legendary lists for December 18. Google’s Academy dashboard shows he can draw an event-linked spike: in its weekly “Past year, US” nominee chart, Chalamet rose from 25 on the March 8 point to 84 on March 15, while Michael B. Jordan rose from 6 to 100. This supports a plausible attention shock and shows a strong rival. It is not a Global Actors rank, annual growth value, or probability.

The primary model’s 18% YES is transparent about weak calibration: 75% weight on ordinary growth at 10% conditional rank-one likelihood; 15% on an outlier regime at 55%; and 10% on a later/different-cutoff regime at 30%, multiplied by 98.5% publication probability and rounded down from 18.47%. These are judgments, not fitted frequencies. The 20-point reserve is a stress test, not a posterior lower bound.

The principal counterweight is the known 2025 method window, January 1–November 25. If repeated in 2026, the December 18 *Dune* release falls outside the precedent window; the market’s March 31 fallback does not extend the measurement period. Google has not published the 2026 method. The model’s 10% later-cutoff weight and 30% conditional likelihood are therefore assumptions. The Academy chart is U.S.-only, spans March 2025–March 2026, covers five nominees, and exposes neither raw counts nor exact query normalization. Its spike cannot be bridged to global annual rank.

The rival set also defeats a Hollywood-only inference. Past Global Actors lists include female and non-Western performers, including Pawan Kalyan and Hina Khan in 2024, alongside diverse international names. Chalamet’s nomination and film promotion do not show that his worldwide year-over-year growth will exceed them. No comparable 2026 Global Actors series is available; Google Trends requests returned 429, an access gap rather than zero interest.

The constructive conclusion is limited: an event-driven Chalamet scenario is plausible, and the model properly avoids treating top-ten absence as disqualification. The sources support a nonzero scenario but do not identify its mixture weights or 18% likelihood.
