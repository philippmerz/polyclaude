# Idaho Achilles follow-up — independent skeptic

**Advisory only, 2026-10-17.** This reviews the primary’s proposed 25/75 ATR/Peak regime weights for frozen case 1870, “Will Todd Achilles win the Idaho Senate race in 2026?” (market 630707; condition `0xd598aae4819d33b840423b82ded0de2a5f583529a1790ff318a4531f98c5c301`). It does not set a probability or authorize an action. No market price was read.

The new Peak evidence improves the case for giving that survey more weight: its report is now directly authenticated as a Peak Insights memo signed by Erik Iverson, and the reported Sep. 28–30 likely-voter, live-phone test is later and closer to the election than ATR’s Sep. 13–16 registered-voter, mixed text/live release. Peak’s five-hundred-person memo reports Risch 49, Achilles 24, Fleming 3, Loesby 2, None 6, and undecided 15. ATR’s original release reports Achilles 45.5/Risch 39.4/Loesby 4.5/unsure 10.6 in its three-way test, and 50.1/38.5/unsure 11.4 in a separate two-way test. The disagreement is not a controlled trend: electorates, modes, field dates, candidate lists, and question context differ.

The 25/75 revision is therefore defensible as a **subjective source-regime prior**, not as calibrated reliability weighting or a statistical blend. Peak’s verification establishes that a branded memo exists; the reported commissioning by the Risch campaign comes from Rubashkin’s post, not the memo image. The memo has no printed date, and neither its questionnaire, wording/order, weighting, response rate, nor biography treatment was recovered. “Four named candidates” should not be called the verified full ballot: the official Idaho candidate portal returned only an app shell, and this packet did not verify final statewide ballot certification. ATR is also a real original poll, with an explicit noncommissioned statement, but its question set omits Fleming and its release does not establish its exact instrument or whether every respondent took both tests.

The weight matters sharply for Achilles’s named-winner claim. In the existing model, the equal-weight branch gives Achilles about 37.6%; the revised run gives about 19.9%; the Peak-only and ATR-only branches give about 1.65% and 74.2%. Those are outputs conditional on the primary’s margins and residual scales, not measured poll probabilities. The 25/75 choice roughly halves the named-claim estimate because it makes Peak-like data validity three times as likely as ATR-like validity. The basis—recency, likely-voter screen, and authenticated memo—is coherent, but it cannot resolve the missing instrument or campaign-sponsorship effects. Preserve the endpoint cases and explain why 25% ATR remains plausible; do not present 19.9% as precise.

The proposed 15-point entry reserve does **not** cover the modeled source-regime loss. Subtracting 15 points from 19.9% leaves about 4.9%, still roughly 3.3 points above the Peak-only 1.65% branch; the stated 1–80% specification range also extends below that stressed center. Thus the reserve is an ordinary policy cushion, not a floor against choosing the wrong survey regime. If a later entry case relies on this mix, it needs either an explicit larger source-regime stress or a concrete reason the adverse regime is implausible, plus current full-ballot and quote evidence. There is no reason to force a “blocked” label solely because the estimate is subjective; the candidacy/ballot gap and the remaining uncertainty should determine whether the primary can finish a useful valuation.

The closest falsifiable crux is whether a transparent, independent, Idaho likely-voter poll using the certified candidate field reproduces either regime. A second current poll or the missing Peak instrument would materially sharpen the weight. The poll’s 15% undecided and 6% “None,” plus possible minor-candidate vote splitting, leave room for the Achilles-vs-Risch margin to misstate Achilles’s chance of winning outright; the model acknowledges that it does not simulate Fleming/Loesby wins. Idaho’s large Republican structural baseline is an adverse failure path for Achilles, while ATR’s positive margin is a genuine counterexample to simply reverting to that baseline.

Achilles’s no-caucus statement is a separate outcome dimension. The campaign-hosted page headline supports stated intent, but no transcript or future commitment is captured; the 5%/5%/90% future caucus allocation is judgment. If Achilles wins, that may change Senate-control/organization outcomes, but it does not change the named-candidate market’s winner criterion. Keep those probabilities separate.
