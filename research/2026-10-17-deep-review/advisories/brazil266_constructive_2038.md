# Case 266: constructive/source-gate review

This is an independent challenge to the existing model, not a replacement forecast. I did not inspect market quotes or economics feeds.

**Best case for the existing YES thesis.** The exact event is a 25 bp decrease in the COPOM target at the scheduled November 3–4 meeting, relative to the premeeting target, with specified rounding. The Oct. 9 BCB Focus R7/2026 record is the most direct forward-looking input found: baseCalculo 0 reports 137 respondents, mean 13.5584%, median 13.5%, range 13.5–14%; baseCalculo 1 reports 48, mean 13.5313%, median 13.5%, the same range. The official 2026 calendar lists November 3–4 as its seventh meeting, matching the R7 label by order. If the premeeting target remains 13.75%, both medians point to the 13.5% target that corresponds to a 25 bp cut. That is substantive support for the central outcome, not a forecast that should be discarded simply because exact event probabilities are unpublished.

The primary also exposes rather than hides its judgment: it uses a 75% “cautious-cut consensus continues” regime with a 78% conditional 25 bp-cut probability, and a 25% information-break regime with 25% conditional cut probability. It includes hold, larger cut and hike outcomes, and discounts for meeting/reference uncertainty. The 20-point reserve can be a useful stress margin for the model’s sparse calibration.

**Why the 63% figure is fragile.** Focus values are respondent expectations of the level, not a distribution of meeting decisions. A 13.5% median and 13.56% mean do not identify the probability of exactly a 25 bp move; hikes, holds and different-sized cuts can combine to the same mean. BaseCalculo codes’ meanings remain undocumented in the captured metadata, so the two groups should not be treated as independent samples. The reported R7 mapping is ordinal; the frozen rule itself supplies the November dates, but the official calendar and communiqué pages returned generic JavaScript shells.

The current-reference gap is decision-relevant. The latest valid captured COPOM decision is September 16 at 13.75%; it says the calibration magnitude will be set as new information arrives and urges caution, with risks skewed upward. September IPCA was 0.82% monthly. These facts make the model’s hold/hike stress branch credible. The Oct. 18 request to the official SGS 432 target-rate API returned HTTP 406 because it requires an explicit date window; no follow-up was made under the request cap. Thus this pass did not prove there was no intervening target change. The official BCB search result gives a September 17 effective date for 13.75%, but the direct communiqué URL also returned only the generic shell.

The result therefore rests on two distinct assumptions: that 13.75% is still the premeeting reference, and that the R7 expectation median maps to a discrete 25 bp decision more often than to a hold/hike mix. The first needs an accessible current official rate record; the second needs either decision-level historical calibration or an explicitly subjective mapping. Upside inflation/fiscal/FX risk is not a reason to erase the cut thesis, but the existing 25% stress-regime weight is judgment and could be too low if premeeting data or COPOM guidance deteriorates. Conversely, a verified unchanged reference plus stable R7 expectations would strengthen the central scenario, not calibrate its exact likelihood.
