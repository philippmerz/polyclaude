## Strongest case for a small NO proposal

The draft assigns 10.7915% to YES (89.2085% NO), with a very wide simulated total: mean 852.7T, median 802.5T, and 5th–95th percentiles 402.8T–1,444.2T. The base estimate’s direction is plausible: the target is a narrow 50T band around 750T, so either continued growth or a sharp contraction often misses it. The model correctly shares a traffic regime across future weeks instead of treating four correlated weeks as independent bets.

A newly recovered public chart series materially improves the evidence. The actual rankings page’s Top Models component calls the no-argument `model-rankings-chart` endpoint; its 53 Monday-labeled points cover Oct 13, 2025–Oct 12, 2026, with nine model series plus `Others`. The last four *completed* chart totals are 128.895T (Sep 14), 145.845T (Sep 21), 165.856T (Sep 28), and 171.843T (Oct 5). Using simple percentage changes, these four levels give three changes: +13.15%, +13.72%, and +3.61% (mean +10.16%, sample SD 5.68%). Across the 12 completed labels Jul 20–Oct 5, the 11 simple changes average +10.69% with sample SD 8.88%. These are not the separate log-return windows used in the root model: that calculation uses five levels Sep 7–Oct 5 for four log returns, and thirteen levels Jul 13–Oct 5 for twelve log returns. The estimators and windows differ. This is observed descriptive variation, not a fitted future distribution.

At the Oct 18 10:34 UTC chart capture, the latest raw point is labeled Oct 12 and sums to 152.318T. Its calendar week was still open, but the chart response provides no observation cutoff for that point. The separate ranking leaderboard says its own usage data run through Oct 17; that date cannot be transferred to the Top Models chart. Thus the point is not a completed weekly total, and its accumulated coverage is unknown. The draft’s cutoff scenarios move case-112 YES only from 10.75% to 11.34%; that small modeled sensitivity does not resolve the source cutoff. The one-regime sensitivities put YES at 17.05% under moderation alone, 8.86% under historical growth alone, 0.46% under boom alone, and 1.40% under disruption alone. Each remains NO-favored, though those are model sensitivities, not empirically calibrated posterior probabilities.

## Strongest YES and cash counter-thesis

The same chart evidence points the other way on the mean. Weekly totals rose from 58.041T on Jul 20 to 171.843T on Oct 5, and the latest three completed-week changes remain positive. If a material share of that growth persists into the four target weeks, the total can overshoot 775T; the draft’s historical-growth-only median is 890.8T. The apparent slowing to +3.61% in the last completed week and the unfinished Oct 12 point leave a credible stabilization story: a roughly 750T total needs an average of 187.5T per future week, only 9.1% above Oct 5’s 171.843T. Modest ongoing growth could land in the YES band rather than above it.

The strongest opposing construction is the concentrated-750T countermodel: with a four-week total centered near 750T and log-sigma 3%, it yields about 73.36% YES. The Monday-labeled weekly history is not a direct estimate of that future four-week fixed-window distribution, so this is not an equal-evidence forecast. But it exposes the draft’s load-bearing judgment: an assumed mean near the target plus low aggregate variance reverses the trade. The draft’s mixture weights (45% moderation, 35% historical growth, 10% boom, 10% disruption), regime drifts/volatilities, shared five-week regime, 0.25 weekly innovation correlation, and additive rounding/revision tails are not fitted or holdout-tested. The observed weekly growth SDs are descriptive samples with trend and possible retrospective revisions; they do not identify future four-week aggregate variance. There are no archived first-display snapshots to test the market’s exact revision/rounding process.

Cash is strongest if the concentrated-target view deserves substantial weight, if the recent growth regime has changed, or if exact chart display mechanics cannot be monitored. The current endpoint sums nine integer series plus `Others`, with no explicit total or recovered exact displayed-total rounding rule. Historical values are current API values, not snapshots of what the chart showed when each week first appeared. Those gaps matter directly to the literal settlement source.

## Pilot economics, if later gates pass

At the dated Oct 17 quote (not current), NO was 0.64 bid / 0.67 ask. A hypothetical five-share passive NO fill at 0.64 risks $3.20. Draft NO 89.21% implies about $1.26 gross expected profit before fees, carry, and execution costs. A 15-point stress gives 74.21% NO and about $0.51 gross expected profit; at 0.68, that falls to about $0.31. If the concentrated countermodel’s YES 73.36% were right, NO at 0.64 loses about $1.87 in expectation. The small full-loss amount can justify a later, strictly capped test only if the primary still believes its stress probability after revisiting the aggregate-volatility challenge and the live quote remains favorable. It does not make the model robust, and the dated book is not an execution certificate.
