# Independent skeptic: Resident Evil box-office range (0183)

The strongest case for YES is mechanical: the frozen range begins at $160m, versus $144.558m cumulative through Oct. 16, leaving 15 dates and an observed recent weekday-retention center near 0.71. The model carries a shared decay regime across those dates rather than pretending daily outcomes are independent. The wide 8–75% sensitivity and 25-point reserve proposal acknowledge that this is a new, poorly calibrated strategy class.

The key near-term anchor is weaker than the report first described. The Numbers has no row-level finality flag; Mojo marks Oct. 16’s $1.6m as estimated. Mojo’s Oct. 16–18 weekend page was still blank, with no Oct. 17 daily actual in either captured title table. Thus the latest matched-Friday ratio and $144.558m starting total are provisional. A weekend opening materially above or below the assumed decay path would move the range probability before any model debate; wait for matched-date actuals and reconcile cumulative totals before treating the current forecast as current.

The decay estimate is fragile at the horizon that matters. Six recent matched weekdays (excluding Monday) are a very short sample, with theater count falling 3,085 to 2,705 over the latest listed week. That does not prove another screen drop, but the recursive model has no explicit theater-count, competing-release, or film-specific audience model. Applying the same latent retention repeatedly makes the projection internally coherent while amplifying regime misspecification: weak retention around 0.55 can strand the total below $160m; sustained retention near 0.71 plus a Halloween lift can clear it. The 70/20/10 regime weights, 0.85 future-Monday factor, Halloween 1/1.4/2 multipliers, and revision-noise mixture are judgmental and lack historical calibration. In particular, a Halloween bump is plausible for the genre, but the 25%/5% uplift weights are not supported by observed title-specific promotion, audience, or comparable-film data.

Source mechanics are unusually clear: the contract uses The Numbers’ Sep. 18–Oct. 31 daily figures, $160m inclusive / $170m exclusive, and demands final figures; if finality is ambiguous, both The Numbers and Mojo must confirm. The Sep. 17 preview is outside the contractual window and should not be added or subtracted. Remaining Oct. 17–31 totals and finality are unknown, not zero. The model’s additive revision noise does not substitute for resolving the estimated-day/final-table issue, and it may understate correlated revisions to daily and cumulative rows.

My main underwriting objection is not that 21% is impossible; it is that the center is highly assumption-sensitive and has no demonstrated box-office calibration or prior-case error record. A 25-point reserve helps against ordinary model error but does not validate hand-set regime weights or protect against a structural break such as a steep screen cut. The small capped pilot could limit loss and collect useful evidence, but it would be an experiment, not an established strategy edge. Treat current-source reconciliation and a fresh two-sided execution/account/correlation check as entry blockers; treat retention, holiday uplift, and forecast calibration as priced risks that still need an explicit downside budget. Do not infer that a maker cap alone creates positive value or a fill.
