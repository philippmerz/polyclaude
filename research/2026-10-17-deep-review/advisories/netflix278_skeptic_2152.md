# Case 278: strongest countermodel to the 31.1% YES draft

The frozen contract is narrow: Netflix’s expected Oct. 20, 3 p.m. ET update for U.S. TV viewership during Oct. 12–18; the exact Boston edition must finish second by weekly chart rank, with alphabetical title as the tie-break. A missed update by Oct. 23, 11:59 p.m. ET resolves Other. The frozen rule does not ask for a current daily rank, a global rank, all-season views, or top-two placement. The draft’s Boston S11 mapping is consistent with Netflix’s official season/release listings. Its 2-of-9 recent seasons reaching #2 is descriptive context, not a calibrated chance for a different season and competition set.

The strongest case for the market’s near-certain YES is a “launch-week cumulative lead” model. Boston S11 released five episodes on Oct. 14, leaving five days in the measured week to accumulate viewing; this is a major franchise rather than an untested title. A cumulative weekly view ranking can preserve an early lead even when the last-day rank slips. The official historical chart records several recent Love Is Blind seasons peaking at #1 or #2; S8 first appeared at #2, and S4 also debuted at #2. This is enough to make a high YES market belief understandable, especially if its participants expect the large first-week episode batch to dominate the week’s total views.

There is limited independent daily evidence in the other direction. FlixPatrol’s title pages, checked Oct. 18, show the series at U.S. daily positions #2, #2, #3, #3 for Oct. 15–18. The Diplomat S4 page shows U.S. positions #3, #2, #2 for Oct. 16–18. Those series-level daily proxy rankings put both releases near the top and suggest a real second-place race. They do not report Netflix views, identify a season-specific U.S. weekly rank, cover the full Oct. 12–18 window, or establish the resolution outcome. FlixPatrol describes its TOP 10 charts as daily and separate from the Netflix weekly view report: [Love Is Blind](https://flixpatrol.com/title/love-is-blind/), [The Diplomat](https://flixpatrol.com/title/the-diplomat/).

The primary model is strongest where it preserves an explicit rank-1 path and a credible rank-2 path under different competitors. Its weakest bridge is the numerical conditional mass: the scenario weights and per-branch rank probabilities are judgments, with no target-week Netflix U.S. view counts or fitted daily-to-weekly mapping. The market can be directionally right if early Boston viewing dominates cumulative totals, but neither the market price nor the proxy establishes that dominance. Conversely, the proxy’s #3 weekend positions do not establish Boston missed #2 weekly.

The decisive evidence is the official Oct. 12–18 U.S. TV weekly chart, read at the frozen update time with the exact title/season mapping and any alphabetical tie. Before then, the highest-value discriminators are official Netflix U.S. weekly views for close prior launch weeks, comparable episode-release calendars, and daily rank history for both contenders; global points or raw franchise-wide rankings cannot substitute. The capture in `work/0278_history_facts_2145.json` is stale at Oct. 4 for the TSV and Oct. 5–11 for the chart. It contains no target-week counts. This memo challenges calibration only; it does not revise the forecast or make an entry decision.
