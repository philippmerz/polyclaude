## Why the central edge can be overstated

The current model is expressly uncalibrated. It maps proprietary Riot GPR scores through a 2024 developer-described 600-point Elo-like formula, while Riot has not established in the cited page that the current 2026 score uses the same mapping or series/game unit. The 50/35/15 branch weights and persistent regional/team shocks (SD 50/65) are chosen, not fitted residuals. Twelve thousand draws reduce simulation noise; they do not validate those assumptions. The very broad sensitivity band (LCK YES 25–80%) is a more honest signal of structural uncertainty than the central Monte Carlo decimals.

The model does correctly sum the four LCK entrants within shared tournament draws and does not count each as an independent chance. But the same-region shock is only one assumed normal component. It does not estimate patch-specific regional adaptation, draft/side effects, roster availability, or a global patch shock that could change league-relative strength. Nor does it establish that the four LCK entries' past GPR strength transfers intact to the new patch. GPR’s global inputs already incorporate prior results; adding the same MSI/finals evidence again as an independent likelihood would double count. The primary correctly says it did not do that.

The strongest evidence for the NO side is that non-LCK teams have live, quantified ratings near the top: BLG 1513, G2 1478 and LYON 1424; the May/July schedule includes BLG defeating HLE 3–1 before HLE won the MSI final 3–2, G2 sweeping GEN in March, and LYON taking HLE to five games. These are useful challenge cases, not independent sample draws or proof of this patch's relative strength. HLE’s MSI win and GEN’s Sept. 3–1 regional-final win remain credible evidence on the other side.
