# BOI October decision: regime and double-counting audit

**Finding:** the 1245 revision does not arithmetically add separate CPI, FX, energy, Citi, and GlobalSource probabilities. It uses a single normalized cut/hold/hike mixture, then a separate 1% no-announcement fallback. The reported `29/68/3` follows that structure. But the regime boundaries are qualitative and overlap; the model does not show that its new 65% cautious weight is estimated independently of the risks already reflected in analyst views.

The exact case is 137 (market 4082027, condition `0x1f5a…c80e3b`, criteria SHA `1cff…d5aa0`): YES means no rate change from the immediately pre-decision level after the Oct 21 BOI decision; a missing decision by the end of the next scheduled meeting falls to “no change.” The forecast is one joint outcome across the BOI hike/cut/hold contracts, not three independent bets.

The original mixture was 70% ordinary disinflation (75/24/1 cut/hold/hike), 25% cautious committee (15/84/1), and 5% new inflation shock (10/50/40). The revision changes these to 30% (75/24/1), 65% (10/89/1), and 5% (5/45/50). Thus the central shift is a transfer of weight from the easing regime to the cautious regime; the shock-regime weight stays at 5%. Its conditional responses also change, so this is not just a copied forecast with CPI/FX add-ons. The explicit 1% fallback is applied once to the joint mixture.

The “Citi forecast” in the entry-preparation packet is a secondary, AI-assisted article published Oct 16; the direct Citi note was not obtained. The 1245 model instead names an Oct 13 GlobalSource analyst teaser as its new evidence, and does not assign Citi a separate branch, likelihood, or vote. That teaser expected a hold while referring to shekel weakness, oil pressure, activity, and fiscal factors; it forecast September CPI near the later official result. The official CBS release then showed headline CPI −0.5% m/m and +1.6% y/y, while seasonally adjusted CPI was flat and rent rose 0.4%. This chronology makes the teaser an alternative analyst judgment under an overlapping information set, not an independent realization of new shock risk. Treating Citi and GlobalSource as two independent confirmations would overstate evidence.

The remaining conceptual risk is real: “cautious committee amid activity/FX/oil and election uncertainty” and “new FX, energy or geopolitical inflation shock before decision” are not defined by mutually exclusive thresholds. October energy/FX conditions were not quantified in the saved primary packet; BOI’s September description of the reviewed-period exchange rate and risk premium is stale. If branch two represents known weak-ILS/oil conditions already priced into an analyst’s conditional hold forecast, branch three must mean a *further* shock arriving before the meeting. The revision does not state that boundary. Its weight transfer can still be a coherent scenario judgment, but not an independent-data update or calibrated likelihood.

**Falsifiers / failure cases:** a fresh BOI inflation-expectations/activity release or committee communication can change the policy state before Oct 21; a material new FX/energy move would test whether the shock branch is genuinely incremental. A pause remains plausible because actual headline CPI undershot the teaser slightly, underlying seasonally adjusted CPI was flat, and the source packets lack current FX/oil levels and direct Citi research. Conversely, stronger local wages/activity, renewed shekel weakness, or energy pass-through could make the cautious hold assessment too low. The 20-point entry reserve is a buffer, not a solution to overlapping branch definitions or source dependence.
