# Case 386 — champion path (evidence challenge only)

The strongest grounded YES path is a *formal personal cessation after the Nov. 29 general election*, not the election result alone. The frozen wording covers Sánchez ceasing to be PM “for any period”; it also says a resignation/removal announcement before deadline qualifies regardless of later effective date. The official 2023 precedent is unusually direct: RD 694/2023 declares Pedro Sánchez’s cessation as President of the Government after the election and in the same sentence says he continues in functions until his successor takes office. That is evidence Spanish practice can distinguish formal personal cessation from substantive caretaker continuity. Whether this venue would treat the analogous 2026 act as YES remains unresolved.

The 2026 timetable makes that branch concrete. RD 806/2026 calls elections for Nov. 29 and Article 5 fixes constituent sessions for Dec. 23 at 10:00, eight days before the contract deadline. The Constitution and Law 50/1997 provide for the outgoing Government to cease after elections and remain in functions until a successor takes office. If an official post-election decree expressly declares Sánchez’s personal cese—as happened in 2023—the literal contract has a plausible YES reading even with caretaker duties continuing. Election calling or chamber dissolution alone is not that act.

The October 7–8 40dB/EL PAÍS flash survey reported PP 31.9%, PSOE 28.1%, Vox 18.9%, and a point seat estimate of 132 PP plus 66 Vox, versus 176 for an absolute majority. This supports an opposition-formation route as a scenario, not a calibrated election, coalition, investiture, or PM-removal probability: it is one 800-person online poll with a reported ±3.5pp sampling error, and its party vote shares, seat projections, coalition willingness, and government formation are different quantities.

The next useful evidence is official and event-specific: final election results; the BOE post-election cese decree and its exact subject/operative wording; official King/Congress investiture schedule and confidence votes; any Sánchez resignation/removal announcement; and the eventual successor’s taking office. A direct qualifying announcement can trigger YES before legal effectiveness. Conversely, a caretaker-only transition without a personal-cese instrument or qualifying announcement would leave the venue interpretation unresolved, not make election loss synonymous with a YES.
