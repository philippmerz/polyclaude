# Independent champion and counter-thesis: Resident Evil box office (0183)

The strongest affirmative case is that this contract buys a well-defined, unusually measurable event at a price that may be far below even a heavily haircut forecast. The frozen rule is specific: The Numbers’ “Daily Box Office Performance” for the 2026 Resident Evil from its September 18 opening through October 31, $160 million inclusive to $170 million exclusive, with the source’s own “domestic” scope. It excludes the separate September 17 preview. The rule also states what to do about finality: wait for both The Numbers and Box Office Mojo if there is ambiguity; after November 30 at 11:59 p.m. ET without final data, choose another credible source. Those terms reduce identity and geographic ambiguity, although they leave settlement timing uncertain.

The Numbers’ latest captured cumulative is $144.558 million through October 16. That leaves $15.442 million to reach the band’s floor across October 17–31, or an average of about $1.03 million per day; the band’s upper edge would require $25.442 million. This is a reachable, not assured, target. The prior October 9–11 weekend made $8.563 million and was marked non-estimated by Mojo; the October 9–12 holiday frame was $9.921 million and also marked non-estimated. A repeatable weekend contribution, modest weekday tail, and some late-month horror demand could put the total inside the band rather than below it. The model’s favorable feature is that it carries one shared retention regime across future days, instead of assuming independent daily decay. Six recent non-Monday matched-weekday ratios cluster around a 0.71 geometric center. If the market is treating the recent decline as a simple binary failure to clear $160 million, it may be missing the band’s middle-outcome payoff and the source’s domestic convention.

At a price no higher than 8¢, the proposed 25-point pilot reserve reduces the primary 46% YES estimate to 21%, still 13 points above the raw purchase price. This is more conservative than the evaluation’s original 15-point reserve, which selected 31%; it does not change that canonical record. A five-share minimum risks 40¢; the 13-share cap risks at most $1.04 before external execution cost. The maker-only premise matters: the recorded 5% linear fee is taker-only, so a genuinely passive fill avoids it; crossing changes the math. A small stake can diversify a portfolio dominated by election, policy, technology and crypto event risk, but no measured cross-strategy correlation or box-office calibration supports hedge credit. This would be an experiment, not a validated edge.

The counter-thesis is strong because the latest starting point is provisional. Mojo marks October 16’s $1.6 million as estimated; The Numbers has no row-level finality flag. Neither captured title table had an October 17 daily actual, and Mojo’s October 16–18 weekend row was still blank. The observed theater count fell from 3,085 on October 9 to 2,705 on October 16, a 12.3% decline. That does not prove future screen loss, but the model has no explicit theater-count, competing-release or film-specific audience mechanism. A weak weekend would leave fewer dates to recover. The last published weekend occurred with more theaters, so it is not a clean forecast analogue.

The Monte Carlo is coherent bookkeeping, not a calibrated box-office posterior. Its 70/20/10 retention regimes, log spreads, future-Monday multiplier, Halloween multipliers, per-day noise and finality-revision mixture are judgments. The six weekday ratios are a tiny, same-title sample; a shared retention draw makes the path internally consistent but magnifies error if the regime changes. The median projection is about $159.9 million, just below the $160 million boundary: small misspecification can move substantial probability mass across the exact lower cutoff. The stated 8–75% range conveys that model uncertainty, but the 25-point reserve is not empirically calibrated and does not guarantee the selected 21% is conservative.

Timing is the main practical weakness. The 14:00 scan reported a YES midpoint near 8.7¢; 15.7¢ was an older ask. Neither is a fresh executable quote. Weekend figures were unpublished in the later source capture, with no promised publication time. A passive 8¢ order could remain unfilled if the weekend is strong, or fill after weak results or screen cuts have already repriced the market. It is not a cheap option safe to leave through the data release. Any entry needs fresh identity, fee, tick/minimum, book, balance and full-fill checks, plus an explicit pull decision before weekend data; a low limit does not solve adverse selection. Capital could remain locked through late November and longer if the fallback source process is delayed; the 44-day carry sensitivity does not cover that further tail.

My champion conclusion is conditional: the source and payoff make a small, capped affirmative pilot intellectually defensible if an actually passive price at or below 8¢ is available before material weekend information, with full loss capped at $1.04 and no add or repost. The case is not strong enough to treat 21% as a robust lower bound or to enter against fresh weak weekend data. The source refresh—rather than an arbitrary calendar rule—should decide whether the opportunity is still the one the model priced.
