# Michigan Senate case 1838 — independent skeptic advisory

The primary has a defensible narrow-Democratic center, but the apparent NO opportunity depends on a close, dated poll and uncalibrated cycle assumptions. This review is advisory; it does not replace the primary estimate or decide entry.

## What supports the primary case

The full contract is a named-party nominee race, not Senate control: YES follows the Democratic nominee, while NO includes every non-Democratic winner. The captured Michigan likely-voter survey is large (n=3,949), asks the actual El-Sayed/Rogers matchup, and reports 49–48 with a stated ±2.5-point share margin. Its direction is compatible with Michigan’s recent close partisan baseline. The model keeps national and Midwest error rather than treating the survey’s sample size as certainty. The official listing also identifies four minor-party nominees, so the literal NO side is broader than Republican success.

## Main vulnerabilities

The polling evidence ends October 4; the evaluation’s review date is October 18. That is a meaningful information gap for a close contest. The source report says the questionnaire varies by voter status and prompts undecided respondents to state a preference before allocation. Thus 49–48 is not a raw, unconditional election-night vote share. The ±2.5 figure describes sampling uncertainty under the poll’s method; it does not measure nonresponse, likely-voter classification, candidate recognition, or late movement. The forthcoming primary source pass should establish whether a newer original survey, questionnaire, candidate withdrawal, debate, or other campaign development changes this record.

The model shifts the structural prior by a subjective −2 points for El-Sayed, then gives the poll about 75% weight and subtracts a judgmental 1.5-point mean poll bias. That produces only about a D+0.65 center and a 55% D win probability. Neither the candidate adjustment nor the average poll bias is Michigan-specific calibration. With the same model, removing the bias penalty changes D to 63.5%; a −3-point penalty changes it to 46.4%. Generic-ballot choices and stacked cycle assumptions also move results materially. These are useful sensitivity disclosures, not evidence that the central inputs are correctly weighted.

The stated 30–80% D range implies 20–70% for NO and is a specification range, not a calibrated interval. A 10-point reserve from the 45% central NO estimate leaves 35%; it cannot protect against the low-NO end of that stated range. This is not a demand that every range endpoint support an entry. The substantive issue is that the entry cushion is small relative to a source cutoff of two weeks, unmeasured candidate effects, and the primary’s own broad range.

All Michigan Senate outcomes also share national-cycle and Midwest shocks with other modeled seats. House and Idaho positions are exposed to much of the same election-wide environment. Per-race Kelly or a separate 10-point haircut does not establish that these are independent risks or quantify their combined drawdown. The primary appropriately says no measured hedge is claimed; combined exposure should remain explicit in any later sizing review.

## Falsifiers and evidence gaps

Before relying on this center, test it against any newer original likely-voter poll with its full wording, field dates, undecided treatment, sponsor, mode, and sample; verify the current six-candidate ballot and any withdrawal or replacement; and check developments that could change turnout or cross-party support. Keep the ordinary-versus-early-vote and election-call/certification timing distinction: the contract requires unanimous AP/Fox/NBC calls or official certification, so an apparent election-night lead is not itself settlement. No current facts here establish a candidate-specific late shift or a calibrated poll-error distribution.

The old book is not used to form or revise this probability. Any eventual price decision must use a new exact-identity quote and current execution checks after the source pass; it should not treat the 55% point estimate or the 10-point reserve as protection from the shared-cycle and source-cutoff risks above.
