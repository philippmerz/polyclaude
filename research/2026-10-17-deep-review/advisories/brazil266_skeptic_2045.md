# Case 266: independent skeptical review

**Question binding.** The frozen rule is the November 3–4, 2026 COPOM decision against the pre-meeting Selic target; it uses the upper bound if the rate is a range. A decrease under 25 bp rounds to a 25 bp decrease, while exactly 37.5 bp rounds to 50 bp, so “Decrease 25” is not every possible easing outcome. A canceled meeting or one delayed beyond the next scheduled meeting resolves No Change; emergency changes do not count. The rule itself supplies the scheduled dates. R7/2026 is plausibly the seventh of the eight meetings in the official indexed 2026 sequence, but the captured BCB calendar responses are JavaScript shells rather than calendar rows. The R7 mapping is ordinal, not independently demonstrated by raw calendar data.

**The Focus signal is informative but not a probability.** On Oct 9, the official Focus R7 aggregate (base 0, n=137) had mean 13.5584, median 13.5, minimum 13.5 and maximum 14 against the last located 13.75 target. Given an unchanged 13.75 reference and treating those reported figures as exact, at least 76.64% of reported point forecasts fall below 13.75; the separate base 1 calculation gives 87.48%. Those are lower bounds on respondents’ forecasts, not on the probability of a 25-bp policy outcome. A median target is not a calibrated event frequency, and the base groups’ code meanings are undocumented and they are not independent samples. The reference itself is only verified at the Sept 16 decision; the latest-rate SGS probe returned an HTML error, so an intervening move has not been ruled out.

The model’s 0.78 “core cut” may be a reasonable judgment if recent Focus forecasts are treated as a strong but imperfect anchor, but there is no backtest or respondent-level distribution-to-outcome map supporting that conversion. Conversely, an actual cut probability above 85–95% is plausible if those clustered targets prove well calibrated and incoming inflation/FX conditions remain compatible with easing. The current data cannot distinguish that case from a much lower probability: survey dispersion describes professional targets, not the committee’s random outcome distribution.

**Regime and information risks.** The 25% break regime is a subjective prior, not estimated from COPOM transitions. Its broad “inflation/fiscal/FX/activity” label risks counting the known September IPCA increase twice: the 0.82% September print was released Oct 9, the same date as the Focus observation. Focus retrieval has a date but no fielding cutoff/time, so whether all responses incorporated that release is unresolved. Separate known news from genuinely subsequent shocks rather than treating a known print as a fresh independent regime event. A hold or hike remains a live counterworld if expectations de-anchor, FX or fiscal risk worsens, or COPOM retains restrictive policy; the Sept 16 statement/minutes explicitly emphasize uncertainty, upside inflation risks and caution. On the other side, the Focus mean/minimum cluster supports a lower target, though not necessarily this exact rounded bracket.

A more defensible presentation keeps the Focus opinion bound separate, then shows conditional cases for unchanged reference / easing-compatible data, persistent hold, and adverse inflation/FX news. The 0.985 meeting-proceeds and 0.99 reference-mapping factors are also judgments; they should not be presented as sourced frequencies. This critique selects no probability or position.
