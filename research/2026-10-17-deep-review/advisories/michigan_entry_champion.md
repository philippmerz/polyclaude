# Independent champion case — Michigan Senate NO (case 1838)

**Research-only advisory, 2026-10-18.** This is a conditional case for keeping a very small NO limit under consideration, not an adopted forecast or order. YES means Democrat Abdul El-Sayed wins; NO is any non-Democratic winner under the frozen rule, not solely a Republican win. Independents do not map to a major party. AP/Fox/NBC must call the same winner or official certification controls. The official Michigan DOS listing currently contains the two major nominees plus Libertarian Lydia Christensen, U.S. Taxpayers Tim Long, Green Douglas P. Marsh, and Natural Law Walter P. Kristy.

The best affirmative argument is source recency and convergence around a genuinely close race. The existing YouGov instrument reports El-Sayed 49 / Rogers 48 among 3,949 Michigan likely voters, fielded Sep. 22–Oct. 4, with ±2.5 share-point MOE; its question varies by voter status and prompts/allocates undecideds, so this is not raw unallocated intention. Newer Rasmussen fieldwork on Oct. 12–14 reports El-Sayed 46 / Rogers 44 among 798 Michigan likely voters, plus Christensen 2, Long 2, Marsh 1, Kristy 0, and 5% unsure; stated MOE ±3 points at 95% confidence. Rasmussen describes a national telephone-and-online survey and says Pulse Opinion Research did its fieldwork. The *population is Michigan likely voters*; “national” is the pollster’s mode description, not a national electorate. Poll-specific mode split, weighting, likely-voter screen, and response rate are not disclosed in the published topline. The pollster describes itself as paying for its polls generally; no poll-specific sponsor is named.

The new D+2 is only one point above the older YouGov D+1. Because pollster, mode, likely-voter model, question handling, dates, and sample differ, that is not a controlled trend or independent replication. It is modest fresh evidence that should inform the primary’s dated revision: as of Oct. 18, the new fieldwork ended four days earlier (Oct. 14), while YouGov ended fourteen days earlier (Oct. 4). It does not by itself establish a 2-point true lead. AP’s Oct. 17 report supplies credible issue context—Gaza, Jewish and Arab American community responses, attacks on El-Sayed, and the Oct. 8 debate—but its anecdotes are not a representative measurement of vote switching, and it reports no candidate health event or withdrawal. The AP direct request returned 403; browser-rendered text was read, so there is no raw AP capture.

Using the prior research draft only to examine the proposed price—not adopting its p—Democratic YES 55% implies NO 45%. At a fee-free passive 33¢ limit for 10 NO shares, central expected payout less stake is $1.20; after the selected 10-point haircut (NO 35%), it is only $0.20 before carry and operating costs. A 33¢ entry needs at least 43% central NO to clear that reserve. The existing 30–80% YES range maps to 20–70% NO, so it includes outcomes where 10 shares lose $1.30 at 33¢; maximum cash loss is $3.30. At the dated Oct. 17 book, NO bid/ask was 31¢/32¢; the 10-share taker walk cost $3.28704 and the draft’s 10-point stressed EV was +$0.21296 before carry/operations. A 33¢ order at that observed 32¢ ask would cross the offer, so it cannot be assumed to earn maker fees; a genuinely post-only route must reject crossing and be rechecked against a fresh book. A later passive fill is not guaranteed.

The favorable pricing claim is therefore narrow: a small limit may be attractive if the primary’s post-Rasmussen revision still clears the 43% reserve threshold, the quote is genuinely passive, and current source, fee, collateral, and whole-fill gates pass. The 2-point stressed cushion is small relative to the uncalibrated 30–80% specification range and model choices. The model’s 2024-state anchor, D+8 generic-ballot transfer, −2 candidate adjustment, poll weighting, and −1.5 cycle-bias assumption are judgments; neither the national environment nor El-Sayed’s personal effect is fitted. A no-bias assumption raises the old D estimate materially, while a stronger negative poll-bias assumption puts D below even odds. The Senate method itself warns that its normal tails are not calibrated.

The proposed $3.30 adds to five existing unfilled orders totaling $10.82 (four House claims and Todd Achilles Idaho), taking reserved pending exposure to $14.12 if all remain. Three existing claims are plainly Democratic outcomes (ME, MT, NY); Michigan may share national-wave and turnout error with them. CA-06 NO and Idaho’s named-candidate claim are distinct, not measured hedges. No portfolio covariance estimate supports treating the Michigan stake as independent. Given a full-loss $3.30 and a two-cent/share stressed buffer, this remains a research lead whose viability depends on the updated primary valuation and execution conditions, not a stand-alone certainty claim.
