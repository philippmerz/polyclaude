# Sedgwick County Senate YES — independent skeptic

**Conclusion:** A tiny passive YES is defensible only conditional on accepting the primary’s near-even county forecast and on a fresh, monitored entry. The strongest evidence is the stable county-versus-state Democratic geography in two different contests: Sedgwick was D+2.288 pp relative to Kansas in 2024 presidential results and D+2.172 pp in the corrected 2022 Senate results. The corrected 2022 county total is D 58,815 / R 90,856 / L 4,446 across 364 precincts; its total row now matches those precinct sums. That supports a modest county advantage over the state environment, not a 2026 candidate or turnout forecast.

The literal event is correctly bound to the Democratic nominee receiving the most valid votes in Sedgwick County’s Nov. 3, 2026 U.S. Senate election, with party replacement, alphabetical last-name tie-break, and an “Other” fallback if results remain indeterminate by March 31, 2027. It is not a statewide winner contract. Hamilton currently precedes Marshall alphabetically in an exact tie. The frozen Gamma outcomes are Yes/No, while the fallback says “Other”; before entry, verify how that fallback maps under this binary market’s adjudication. Until then, the model’s 1% “Other or delayed” mass is an unresolved settlement-mapping risk as well as a small election-probability branch.

The forecast’s main vulnerability is the jump from county history to a close 2026 Kansas race. The state estimate leans heavily on two close but already-aged statewide polls: Emerson fielded Sept. 15–16 and Trafalgar Sept. 29–Oct. 1; the latest is about 17 days old at the case evaluation. Neither measures Sedgwick. Trafalgar’s reported response rate was 5.5%, while its full interview/weighting description was not recovered; Emerson’s source has a panel/text-to-web mix, and the linked detailed instrument was not available. Final general-ballot and minor-candidate status also remained unverified in the valuation. The state model applies an unbacktested poll weight and common-error structure, then a centered local SD of 5 pp. Its own stacked adverse-state plus −5 pp local-shift scenario puts D below the proposed maker price: D 18.2%, versus 20–23¢, so expected value is negative in that scenario. This does not disprove the 50% center, but it means the 15 pp reserve does not cover every adverse case the model itself displays; the 15–85% range’s lower end also fails at 20¢.

At a 20¢ fill, five shares cost $1.00, win for $5, and can lose the full $1.00. At the 23¢ hard cap, five shares cost $1.15, win for $5, and can lose the full $1.15. Under the primary’s supplied 50% point, expected profit is $1.50 at 20¢ and $1.35 at 23¢; after its 15 pp reserve (p=.35), those become $0.75 and $0.60 before carry and operating cost. The model’s 18.2% adverse-grid case instead loses about $0.09 or $0.24 in expected value on five shares. The proposed $1.05 deploy amount is not a strict $1.05 risk cap: the vetted helper rounds shares, so at 23¢ and the five-share venue minimum it can sign $1.15; the separately stated $1.20 ceiling is therefore the binding cash limit. Taker-only fee status supports zero fee only on actual post-only fills; the 00:20 book observation is dated, and a resting fill can arrive after adverse information. Near-term debate/early-voting timing in the case needs source-confirmed dates and pull coverage.

**Conditions that would strengthen or falsify the entry thesis:** a new original poll with Sedgwick cross-tabs or county fieldwork would directly test the transfer; an official final ballot, withdrawal, or party-status change could alter vote splitting; fresh statewide polling that moves Hamilton materially behind would pressure the state center; a verified shift in early-vote/campaign behavior in Sedgwick would pressure the local lean. Before any post, refresh exact market identity, fees, touch, ticket sizing, collateral, cluster headroom, monitoring coverage, and event pull timing. Treat this as correlated U.S. election exposure alongside the existing Senate and House bids, not as a diversifier or a hedge against them.
