# Case 262: independent model critique

The frozen condition is narrow: Chalamet must rank #1 in Google’s official 2026 **Global Actors** Year in Search list. Google’s 2025 methodology describes fastest year-over-year growth in interest over Jan 1–Nov 25, not absolute search volume. The 2026 list controls even though the market question says “searched”; if Google does not publish it by Mar 31, 2027, the contract falls to Other/NO. The 2025 cutoff is precedent, not proof of the 2026 cutoff or publication.

The 18% working prior is arithmetically coherent but not empirically estimated. Its .985 publication probability and the .75/.15/.10 regime weights and conditional .10/.55/.30 ranks are subjective inputs. The facts do not estimate any of them: Chalamet was outside the displayed Global Actors top ten in 2023–25, but the published top tens censor ranks below ten; three winners (Renner, Katt Williams, Mikey Madison) do not calibrate next-year rank. The alternative Google Academy chart is U.S., weekly, “Past year,” and ends Mar 15; its normalized values are neither raw searches nor global annual growth. Its Chalamet/Michael B. Jordan comparison cannot support the contract’s rank.

A materially lower outcome remains plausible if Google retains the early cutoff and another actor has greater global YoY growth. The record lacks complete comparison candidates, baseline query volumes, a global 2026/2025 series, and the 2026 cutoff. A result below 1–2% would require very low probability that Chalamet leads the eventual list (or meaningful nonpublication/Other risk); current evidence cannot quantify that tail. Conversely, a result above 10% is possible if his global year-over-year growth leads, or if the measurement window extends into the December *Dune: Part Three* campaign/release. The March Oscar attention and December film are real events, not measured search-growth effects. The frozen fallback makes publication uncertainty consequential, but the .985 factor also lacks a historical release-frequency estimate.

Partial identification is more defensible than the point arithmetic: the official evidence fixes the target metric, one prior-edition method/cutoff, three censored historical top tens, and a U.S. award-week proxy. It does not identify a numeric 2026 ranking probability. Keep the 18% as a transparent judgment only if its regime weights are explicitly treated as subjective and stress-tested; do not describe it as data-calibrated. The cited 20-point reserve is a separate decision haircut, not a confidence interval. This critique does not assess quotes or entry economics.
