This is the new CA-04 contest, not the old district or the June primary result.

For U.S. House, California calls this a voter-nominated office. The state’s current guidance says the top two primary vote-getters advance irrespective of preference, and a write-in candidate cannot run for a voter-nominated office in the general election ([California SOS, Primary Elections](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/primary-elections-california), [SOS official 2026 voter guide](https://vigarchive.sos.ca.gov/2026/primary/voter-info/elections-ca.htm)). Thus, if the two listed candidates remain the exhaustive winner set and the market applies their ballot-listed Democratic designation at its call trigger, every winning path maps to Yes. This is a finite-set/contract-interpretation thesis, not a “solid D” label or an electoral probability estimate.

## Gaps that block treating the implication as unconditional

- **Party label is not party affiliation under California law.** SOS says a voter-nominated candidate’s party-preference designation is selected by the candidate, is shown for voter information only, and does not imply party endorsement or affiliation ([SOS voter-nominated offices](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/voter-nominated-offices)). The contract instead says “ballot-listed or otherwise identifiable affiliation” *at the time all House races are called*. That phrase likely makes the ballot label relevant, but the state source does not establish that market adjudicators must equate preference with affiliation, nor that either candidate’s status cannot change before the trigger. The special caucus-intent rule applies only to a candidate without a ballot-listed affiliation to either major party.
- **Roster scope:** Yolo and Sacramento are independent official county election-office checks, not a statewide certified November roster. The statewide SOS certified list found in this review is for the June primary. The election offices agree on the named general candidates, but a stronger claim should be checked against the official general ballot/candidate record for the other district counties if available.
- **Replacement/cancellation:** California’s official SOS material states the top-two/write-in rules. Chaptered AB 1413, the official amendment text for Elections Code §8803, says a vacancy on a voter-nominated general-election ballot is not filled; a deceased candidate stays on the ballot, votes count, and a majority elects the candidate (then the seat is vacant) ([chaptered AB 1413](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/11-12/bill/asm/ab_1401-1450/ab_1413_bill_20120210_chaptered.html)). This reduces ordinary replacement risk but does not settle every contract interpretation, court-order, or election-administration contingency. The market text itself provides no cancellation/refund clause in the frozen criteria reviewed.
- **Resolution time:** Gamma’s scheduled `endDate` is `2026-11-04T04:59:00Z`; its criteria instead await calls of all House races. SOS gives December 3 as the county certification deadline and December 11 for the certified Statement of Vote ([SOS 2026 general-election calendar](https://www.sos.ca.gov/elections/upcoming-elections/general-election-november-3-2026/key-dates-deadlines)). Those dates do not predict platform resolution, but show that the contractual trigger can outlast election night. Carry/opportunity cost and current available funds remain root’s checks.

## Doctrine implication for root
