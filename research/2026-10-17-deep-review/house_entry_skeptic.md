# Skeptical review — NC11 / OH07 NO maker candidates

**Scope:** cases81 and116; critique of the primary estimates and proposed small maker quotes, not an alternative forecast or trade instruction. The corrected sensitivity ranges are NC11 0.30–0.95 and OH07 0.35–0.95. The contemplated 20-share quotes cost at most 3.00 and 3.40 pUSD respectively (6.40 combined, about 3.3% of 196 NAV); this limits dollars at risk but does not establish positive expected value or independent risk.

The central 70%/72% estimates are reproducible outputs, but their precision exceeds the calibration evidence. The 8pp national generic-ballot anchor is a single dated Economist/YouGov poll whose 7/8/11pp results depend on screen; choosing 8 is defensible as a scenario, not a stable district baseline. Cook PVI's R+5 is a two-party margin differential, so doubling the displayed five-point share is conceptually appropriate. However, multiplying that differential by an assumed 0.8 transfer is not estimated here, and the model does not show whether the national generic measure and PVI adjustment are calibrated together or partially double-count national partisanship. The broad beta/national/poll-bias grid correctly exposes this sensitivity, but its endpoints are not probabilistic coverage intervals.

The poll combination is especially fragile. NC's Sep10–11 PPP result is old, field/sample/mode details rely on secondary reporting, full questionnaire is unavailable, and The Bench partnership raises a sponsor-selection concern. OH's Aug4–5 Tavern result is older still: its named-candidate q11 is 54–46, while generic q10 is 50–50. That difference may reflect candidate recognition or question context; treating q11 as an 8-point district-margin observation risks mistaking a prompt-specific contrast for durable vote intention. The stated 635 is the poll's overall sample; follow-up bases 312+248=560 can explain the secondary 560 without implying a contradictory total. Neither result should be counted alongside same-period partisan polls as independent evidence. Added non-sampling and age/turnout SDs acknowledge weaknesses, but 3pp/2.5pp for NC and 3pp/5pp for OH are judgmental inputs, not empirically validated corrections; poll-selection and sponsor bias may be directional rather than zero-mean noise.

Cash is useful context, not a vote conversion. NC's Q3 cash/receipts advantage supports Ager's capacity, but the +1.5pp bonus is unfitted and omits outside spending and later cash. OH's +1pp adjustment is similarly speculative; Miller's $1m loan is included in his $1.147m Q3 receipts, not an additional $1m. Miller's incumbency −2pp is also an assumed effect. These signs may be reasonable, but the thinness of OH's stressed maker edge makes them decision-sensitive.

The .15 NC and .17 OH quotes are hypotheses, not live execution certificates. OH's reported 10pp-stress cushion is only about 0.20 pUSD before fees, carry, adverse selection, partial fills, and delay to the all-House-call resolution trigger. Both NOs share Democratic-wave and resolution risk; their combined cap is not diversification. Fresh polls, exact settlement/affiliation treatment, current depth and executable costs remain material checks before any entry.
