## Strongest contrary evidence / residual tails

1. **Ballot preference is not formal party membership.** SOS expressly says voter-nominated candidates are nominees of the people, not official party nominees; the candidate-selected preference is for voter information only and is not endorsement or necessarily an affiliation. The contract’s “ballot-listed or otherwise identifiable affiliation” language leaves an interpretation seam if a winner makes a conflicting affiliation/caucus announcement before all House races are called. Its caucus fallback is expressly for a candidate *without* a ballot-listed Democrat/Republican affiliation, which neither present nominee appears to be, but “otherwise identifiable” is broader than the county’s DEM label. I found no source establishing either candidate intends to change affiliation/caucus or that the market has a binding precedent for this clause; do not assume such a change.
2. **The ordinary third-candidate route is foreclosed; later replacement is a residual legal question.** SOS says only candidates who were top two at the primary may run in the general, and no independent/write-in route directly to the general exists. I did not verify an official current statute or certified all-county ballot governing a hypothetical post-primary death, disqualification, or court-ordered replacement, nor the primary finish order needed to establish whom a vacancy rule would place on the ballot. Treat a later legal ballot change as a low-frequency but unquantified tail, not as an existing third-candidate option. No evidence found that such an event is pending.
3. **Settlement timing/source risk remains.** The end date is not an explicit guarantee of payout at that instant: party classification is keyed to when *all 2026 House races* are conclusively called. A delayed contest elsewhere can hold this condition open. “Consensus of credible reporting” is not a named fixed panel; FEC is the contract’s ambiguity fallback, although it is not the county/state certifying authority. No special cancellation/void rule appears in the frozen description; platform-level resolution discretion/cancellation and FEC reporting timing were not independently measured here.
4. **Map and coverage are mostly resolved, not perfectly redundant.** SOS confirms Prop. 50 map timing and Yolo’s official elections office gives the exact district heading and nominees. This is much stronger than relying on a candidate-name search or a county statement page. I did not retrieve the all-counties certified sample ballot or official state-wide certified candidate roster for independent cross-check; Yolo reports districtwide federal candidates for a district that spans counties, but its page alone does not prove its listed candidates are the only districtwide nominees. SOS’s official Candidate Information/Contact List link is available from Yolo but this check did not download the large PDF.

## Reserve question for root

The two-DEM closed-ballot proof is substantive contrary evidence to a generic 10-percentage-point *election outcome* buffer; under the stated ballot and rule assumptions the party result is mechanically fixed, not an ordinary polling forecast. However, it does **not** quantify affiliation-interpretation, changed-ballot/legal, source/adjudication, cancellation, or settlement-delay tails. I found no measured historical rate or market-specific calibration for these tails. So the facts support considering a narrower-than-generic buffer **conditional on** verifying the certified ballot/current nominee status and resolving which party classification the rule will actually use; the facts alone do not support assigning a particular smaller numeric reserve or asserting a measured tail below the existing 10pp default. Root should not treat 10pp as evidence of election risk, nor reduce it solely because the market is a logical payoff.
