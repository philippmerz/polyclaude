## Official evidence, including the contrary case

The [September 18 decision](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmdeci/mpr_2026/k260918a.pdf), read in full, raised the overnight-call guideline to 1.25% effective September 24, vote 7–2. It describes a moderate recovery, rising cost/wage pass-through and continued conditional tightening. The dissenters preferred holding because inflation and economic strength did not justify that step. A 7–2 vote for a recent 25 bp move is not a 7/9 probability of a fresh 50 bp move.

The [October 1 Summary of Opinions](https://www.boj.or.jp/en/mopo/mpmsche_minu/opinion_2026/opi260918.pdf), covering the September meeting and read in full, contains both an argument for faster tightening if upward price deviations emerge and arguments against haste. It notes September's hike already shortened the interval since June. Moderate pass-through and resilient capital demand make an upside response possible; soft domestic demand and uncertainty around neutral rates favour observation. These are edited summaries, not a named vote distribution for October.

[National CPI](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/cpi/sokuhou/tsuki/index-z.htm) latest at the observation date is **August**, 1.9% headline and 1.7% ex-fresh-food year over year. [Tokyo September's preliminary bulletin](https://www.stat.go.jp/data/cpi/sokuhou/tsuki/pdf/kubu.pdf) is hotter at 2.7% headline/ex-fresh and 3.0% ex-fresh/energy, but local water tariffs and childcare policy affect its composition; it is not September national CPI. National September CPI is scheduled for October 23, a direct upcoming catalyst. [MH LW August provisional wages](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/toukei/itiran/roudou/monthly/r08/2608p/dl/houdou2608p.pdf) report nominal total cash earnings+3.8% and scheduled pay+3.9%, supporting wage pass-through. The figures are provisional and do not mechanically compel a double-size hike.

## Primary probability construction

I allocate about 20% to any additional October hike after September's recent move, and 7.5% conditional on a hike to a move above 25 bp after rounding, yielding 1.5%. The first weight balances inflation/FX upside against the short interval and weak domestic-demand evidence. The second reflects a central-bank preference for gradual moves and the absence of a captured explicit double-size warning, while retaining an import-price/yen or communication shock. Both weights are judgmental, not fitted frequencies, and the 0.1–8% range admits a much more aggressive interpretation of Tokyo/wages or a fresh policy signal. No current OIS distribution or policy-maker commitment was verified; no market probability supplies these weights.

The key distinction is between **direction and size**. Evidence making a 25 bp hike more likely does not raise this bucket equally. A shock can also cause an inter meeting move, resetting the anchor so the eventual meeting change is smaller. The next national CPI and official BOJ speeches matter more than assuming all current inflation observations persist unchanged for two weeks. The horizon is short, but the rare policy response is not a measured binomial tail and does not qualify for a K5 exception.
